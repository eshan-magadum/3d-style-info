# 01 — His Tattoo Turned Ironic After Amputation

**Duration:** 35.37 s · **Frames:** 1,059 · **Source:** 360×640, 29.97 fps · **Mode:** emotional irony and recovery

## Story / narration structure

Caption-led narrative opens on the tattoo “You'll Never Walk Alone,” then immediately creates a contradiction: the soldier loses part of his leg in Afghanistan and the amputation removes the word “Alone.” The ironic wording becomes a bridge into recovery: prosthetic, walking, running, races, and medals. The ending returns to the tattoo and proves the phrase wrong through action. The structure is clue → cause → ironic reveal → rebuild → visual payoff.

The captions are explanatory and causal. The images often take the emotional role: explosive danger, close surgical contact, repeated rehabilitation, and public running success. Maintain narration-to-image agreement: an injury line should show the cause or immediate result, a recovery line should show a new skill, and the gold-medal line should show the medal/race outcome.

## Craft observations

- **Lighting and grade:** strongest feature is the deliberate transition from hot sandy battle tones to cool surgery/hospital tones and a brighter athletic finish. Overall frame-average luma is 0.466 and saturation is 0.449; do not use those as target settings for every shot.
- **Depth:** macro subject isolation, open landscape scale, foreground surgical hands, and rehabilitation room layers make each phase feel distinct. Keep the tattoo readable when it is the evidence.
- **Camera:** frequent pushes/tracks and scale changes align with changes in narration. Preserve a stable view of the tattoo for the irony line; use momentum and blur only on motivated transitions.
- **Texture/outline:** ink, skin, prosthetic, metal, dust, fabric, and ground are the key texture targets. The design is shaded full 3D with no consistent comic-ink outline treatment.
- **Expression/realism:** expression progresses from confident soldier to pain/strain to determination and pride. The rendering is cinematic CGI, with a few stylized or synthetic anatomy details. Prioritize believable weight shifts and leg support over extra skin detail.
- **Best transferable idea:** make one physical clue carry both the hook and final payoff; return to it after the emotional transformation.

## Risks to watch in a new version

Keep laterality and prosthetic shape consistent. Make the amputation happen at the same anatomical level in every view. Do not let the tattoo lettering mutate except where the script intentionally changes the visible message. At the race, keep the runner's gait and the prosthetic contact believable. Avoid graphic detail beyond what is needed for the story.
