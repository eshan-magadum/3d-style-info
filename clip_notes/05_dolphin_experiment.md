# 05 — The Woman Who Lived With A Dolphin

**Duration:** 39.47 s · **Frames:** 1,184 · **Source:** 720×1280, 29.97 fps · **Mode:** historical experiment and tragedy

## Story / narration structure

The source captions describe a woman filling a room with water until a dolphin could swim, moving in with him, painting her face white and outlining her lips so he could watch her mouth, and repeating words. Bright green “HI”/sound text visualizes the attempt and imitation. The story then pivots to the experiment losing funding; a calendar compresses the months they lived together; the dolphin is moved to a smaller, darker tank, gets worse, is separated from the woman, and dies. The final causal chain is intimacy/hope → repeated training → institutional shutdown → poor conditions/separation → death. The synopsis is based on visible captions and images; historical accuracy is not verified by this visual analysis.

## Craft observations

- **Lighting/grade:** cool blue environmental base, hard window patches, bright water reflections, and selective saturated graphics. Mean luma is 0.316; saturation 0.430. The darker scenes must preserve the dolphin silhouette and waterline.
- **Depth:** pool reflections foreground, people/props in the middle, windows and walls in the back. Underwater/surface planes, the smaller tank, and changing scale make confinement visible.
- **Camera:** continuous pushes and pans connect the flooded room; object-centered closeups carry key facts. The calendar and tank move are effective time/scale transitions. Keep camera motion slow enough for the narration and text to read.
- **Texture/outline:** water, ripples, wet floor, dolphin skin, makeup, rope, wood, paper, and fabric are the critical surfaces. There is no outline-driven look. The woman is smooth and stylized; close facial acting is more graphic than photoreal.
- **Expression/realism:** face goes from focused teaching to hopeful surprise, then subdued grief. Dolphin movement and contact with water are central realism tests. Avoid sliding on the floor or a dolphin appearing to hover above the surface.
- **Best transferable idea:** let one environment evolve with the story—shared room, then a restricted tank—so location itself becomes the emotional consequence.

## Risks to watch in a new version

Keep the water level, room layout, window direction, hose, dolphin scale, woman's outfit, and tank geometry continuous. Give the dolphin buoyancy, drag, and wake; reflections should match motion. Render words as controlled graphics and pin them to screen or world space deliberately. Verify all historical statements and respectful treatment of the real people/animal before publishing.
