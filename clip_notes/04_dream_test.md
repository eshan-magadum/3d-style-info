# 04 — How To Tell If You're Dreaming

**Duration:** 36.24 s · **Frames:** 1,087 · **Source:** 720×1280, 29.97 fps · **Mode:** surreal information demo

## Story / narration structure

The narration gives a practical reality-check premise: if you think you might be dreaming, find text and try to read it. The visuals first show a young man in a bright rooftop café, then a poster/page whose writing appears jumbled, then a sequence of readable text, a menu, food/service imagery, and a bedroom. The middle argues that reading and understanding can feel convincing even when a scene is a dream; the ending asks what is real. Caption fragments visible in the source include “find some text,” “looks jumbled,” “you're probably not,” “seeing real text,” “and comprehending,” “based on the context,” “the dream,” “without remembering the words themselves,” “your brain could fake reading like that,” and the closing “what's real?” These are read from burned captions and are not a verified audio transcript.

## Craft observations

- **Lighting/grade:** a clean daylight rooftop is contrasted with navy bedroom scenes and warm restaurant/wood scenes. Mean luma is 0.413; saturation 0.330; edge density 0.0415 (lower than the 360×640 samples, partly because this is a different scene type and resolution).
- **Depth:** repeated portrait, sign/page, bed, table, and background layers guide the viewer through scenes that cannot be physically continuous. Deliberate parallax and object match are the glue.
- **Camera:** gentle portrait push, close reads, and an unusually expressive rotate/tilt/morph create dream logic. The strongest movement must be eased and connected to the set, not a three-frame snap.
- **Texture/outline:** smooth skin and fabric, glass, paper/text, bedding, wood, food, and a bright brain overlay. No consistent outlines. The changing text is an intentional visual clue; avoid accidental gibberish unless explicitly scripted.
- **Expression/realism:** the man is calm and curious, then focused and slightly confused; small eye/head changes carry performance. Smooth skin and limited facial motion make this polished stylized CGI, not live-action realism.
- **Best transferable idea:** create an information graphic inside the narrative world—a readable prop or body visualization—so exposition stays visual.

## Risks to watch in a new version

Typography is central: author the text as a separate graphic/texture and control the exact change. Lock the character identity across every environment. The bedroom geometry, bed orientation, and camera axis must stay consistent through the rotation. If the ending's surreal imagery contradicts the explanatory narration, make the metaphor explicit in voiceover or simplify the scene.
