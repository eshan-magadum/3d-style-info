# 02 — Mother Fights Leopard To Save Her Son

**Duration:** 37.44 s · **Frames:** 1,121 · **Source:** 360×640, 29.97 fps · **Mode:** survival action

## Story / narration structure

The clip begins at the point of danger: a leopard attacks and drags the boy away. The mother sees the threat and pursues him through the dark; she finds him pinned and has only a stick. Her first strike buys a chance to pull the child free, but the leopard turns on her. Villagers arrive with torches, and the story closes on the survivors in a hospital. This is a danger → pursuit → vulnerability → desperate action → renewed threat → community rescue → relief ladder.

## Craft observations

- **Lighting/grade:** the strongest identity is blue night forest balanced with warm daylight at the start and torchlight at the rescue. It is the darkest and most saturated clip by frame averages (mean luma 0.286; mean saturation 0.507). Retain detail in fur and clothing; do not crush faces into blue-black.
- **Depth:** grass and branches make foreground layers; the path and tree trunks make a middle plane; cool haze separates distant foliage. The overhead map briefly turns geography into readable evidence.
- **Camera:** the opening attack is abrupt and close, then the search follows the mother. A map insert gives the audience a breath before returning to action. Fast impact moments feel forceful but should never hide exactly who is protecting whom.
- **Texture/outline:** leopard fur, rough ground, leaves, sari cloth, wood stick, brick, and torch smoke are focal materials. There is no consistent black outline; visual separation comes from rim light and value contrast.
- **Expression/realism:** mother performance must progress from alarm to resolve to strain to relief. Child should remain vulnerable and spatially connected to her. Leopard movement needs believable mass, paw contact, and head/shoulder mechanics; generated action can otherwise look like a pose sequence.
- **Best transferable idea:** build danger around a clear spatial problem; each escalation changes the mother's available choices.

## Risks to watch in a new version

Lock animal size, coat markings, mother wardrobe, child position, stick hand, attack direction, and rescue geography. Establish the boy's location before quick action. Do not let impact flashes cover contact. Keep the map legible and its direction consistent with the forest path. Treat the rescue as a staged reenactment and verify factual claims independently if used as nonfiction.
